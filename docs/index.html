Pursue Your Question in Depth

PhD in Philosophy · standalone or conjoint with the VU · part-time available

A doctorate for those ready to make an original contribution, whether you are preparing for a university career or pursuing advanced study for other serious reasons.

The full-time pathway: about five years

  1. About 2 years Coursework, the literature review and proposal, languages, and the candidacy examination.
  2. About 3 years Dissertation research, writing and oral defence.

Part-time. You can study part-time from the start, including during coursework. A part-time pathway takes longer; plan it with the Program Director.

Questions on the table

Every seminar starts from a question worth a semester. These come from this year’s and recent course descriptions.

Scholarship as a Practice of Wisdom

ICS resists scholarship that retreats into narrow specialization. Here, rigorous thinking is asked to answer to life: to persons, churches, institutions and the culture we share.

  • Faith shapes the questions. Christian faith is not added to otherwise neutral scholarship. It shapes what we ask, how we work and what the work is for.
  • Spiritual depth, public relevance. Scholarship that is spiritually serious and still practicable: able to address cultural crises with wisdom.
  • Across the disciplines. Philosophy, theology and biblical studies, with aesthetics, politics, education and hermeneutics, in one conversation.
  • Small seminars, close supervision. At ICS, students are called Junior Members and faculty are Senior Members. The names say what the relationship is: learning here is apprenticeship, not transaction. You read, argue and write alongside Senior Members, and one of them mentors you through the whole degree.

  1. “Is “God” a male word? Is the “Word made Flesh” a male God?”

    From God/Sex/Word/Flesh, Nik Ansell, scheduled Fall 2026

  2. “…religion must be understood as shaping how we experience the world and not simply as a distinct kind of experience…”

    From Material Spirituality, Neal DeRoo, scheduled Winter 2027

  3. “How does Pragmatism’s suspicion of traditional “supernaturalist” theology influence how we think about God, religion, and human responsibility?”

    From Pragmatism, Race, and Religion, Ronald A. Kuipers, recently taught Winter 2026

  4. “How can we read and experience the Scriptures as the Word of Life in the midst of an Academy that believes the biblical witness will restrict human freedom and thwart our maturity?”

    From Biblical Foundations, Nik Ansell, scheduled Fall 2026

  5. “…to understand and evaluate the phenomenon that is “post-truth” and the anxieties—but also the possibilities—that it represents.”

    From IDS, Neal DeRoo and Nik Ansell, recently taught Winter 2026

Study from where you are

The seminars are small and meet live online, so you take part from home, from work or from another country.

Online
Every MA and PhD seminar in the 2026–27 catalogue meets online, live, at a set weekly time. Times are Eastern Time (Toronto). Times on this site also show in your own time zone.
Part-time
If you work full-time, ICS recommends one course a term. You can take a couple of courses for credit before committing to a program, and they can later count toward the degree.
Full-time
Full-time study means six semester courses a year, for example three in the fall and three in the winter.
From abroad
You can take either program from outside Canada. ICS cannot currently offer student visas, so international students study online. VERIFY Academic Office (current international-student and visa wording) — VERIFY BEFORE PUBLICATION

Seven fields, overlapping like neighbourhoods

Rather than dividing philosophy into its sub-disciplines, ICS gathers courses into interdisciplinary fields. Think of them as overlapping neighbourhoods: each is home to a discipline that informs, and is enriched by, its neighbours. Each field works as a concentration within which you find your own path.

  • Aesthetics, Hermeneutics, and Philosophy of Discourse

    The arts, culture, language, rhetoric and interreligious dialogue.

  • Anthropology and Ethics

    Community, ecology, embodiment, gender and social critique.

  • History and Historiography of Philosophy

    Medieval, modern and contemporary thought, from Aquinas and Kant to Arendt, phenomenology, Critical Theory and Reformational philosophy.

  • Knowledge, Truth, and Learning

    Education and schooling, epistemology and metaphysics, faith and philosophy.

  • Philosophy of Religion and Philosophical Theology

    Faith and reason, secularization, religious practice in the public sphere, and religious language.

  • Social and Political Philosophy

    Civil society, cultural pluralism, democracy, empire, feminism, globalization, and faith and politics.

  • Theology and Biblical Studies

    Theological aesthetics and hermeneutics, Christology and eschatology, spirituality and worship, and faith and culture.
