Junior Members

At ICS, students are called Junior Members and faculty are Senior Members. The names say what the relationship is: learning here is apprenticeship, not transaction. Here is how Junior Members study, the resources they use, and the kind of work they write.

Already a Junior Member? The Handbook, catalogue, library and repository are at the foot of this page.

How Junior Members study

Seminars
All ICS courses are seminars: small groups reading and arguing together with a Senior Member. Every MA and PhD seminar in the 2026–27 catalogue meets online, live, at a set weekly time. Times are Eastern Time (Toronto).
A mentor
Each Junior Member works with a Senior Member mentor, who helps choose electives and guides the thesis or dissertation.
Pace
Full-time study means six semester courses a year, for example three in the fall and three in the winter. If you work full-time, ICS recommends one course a term.
Beyond ICS
Up to two courses from the Toronto School of Theology can count toward an ICS degree.

What Junior Members write

Every MA ends in a thesis and every PhD in a dissertation, written with a mentor and defended orally. Recent examples are listed on the alumni page, and the full collections are in the ICS repository.
